Authentic or replica?

Every club sells two versions of the same shirt, and the price gap is large.

  • Replica is the one most fans buy. Looser cut, heavier fabric, same badge and sponsor. Comfortable to wear to the pub or the match.
  • Authentic (sometimes called "player issue" or "match") is what the players actually wear. Tighter, lighter, more technical fabric, and usually a good deal more expensive.

Unless you're playing in it, replica is the sensible buy. The authentic version mainly costs more for a cut that's less forgiving.

Getting the size right

Football shirts run smaller than normal t-shirts, and authentic versions run smaller again. If you're between sizes, or buying authentic, size up. Check the retailer's size guide rather than assuming your usual size carries over, since Nike, adidas, Puma and Castore all cut differently.

When new kits come out

Home kits usually land in late spring or early summer, before the season starts. Away and third kits follow through the summer and sometimes into autumn. If you want a kit cheap, the time to buy is when its replacement is announced — the outgoing shirt gets discounted hard.

Name and number printing

Official Premier League printing costs extra on top of the shirt, and it's usually non-refundable once applied. Worth thinking twice before putting a player's name on a shirt in January, when he might be at another club by February.
